# Warm-route locality: finite source feasibility review

Decision: **GO for a narrow source design only; NO-GO for the existing 48-row diagnostic unchanged.** A route-local preference for an idle pooled transport is compatible with the original four-lane, both-direction warmup requirement. A physical-lane affinity rule alone is not. No implementation, tests, build or traffic is authorized by this review. The early-share candidate remains unpromising and incomplete.

## Mechanism and limits

The current server chooses a physical lane by round robin before looking up its lane-specific HTTP transport (`agent_transport_pool.go:109`, `agent_tunnels.go:90`). Aggregate public requests use lanes 1–4, while legacy public requests use lanes 0–3. Aggregate lane 0 is reserved for trusted control. Thus a sequential same-route request can bypass a reusable origin connection and its physical sender merely because a different lane is next. Existing pool entries already retain the exact route/origin/TLS/timeout key, agent generation pointer, in-flight body ownership and an advisory idle hint.

A preference for an existing usable same-route entry could reduce origin reconnections and physical-sender revisit gaps. This is a locality hypothesis, not proof that lane scheduling caused the regression. `lastUsed` records request start, not completion or TCP activity; `idleHint` is sticky and advisory. Neither proves a warm congestion window. Even one physical lane can have long directional idle periods while download and upload alternate. Shortening a revisit from several transfers to one opposite-direction transfer need not avoid idle restart, restore low ssthresh or prevent application-limited window reduction. Do not implement TCP keepalive traffic, select by observed throughput, or promise that this mechanism repairs the kernel behavior.

## Why the original warmup can remain unchanged

`scripts/qualify-proxy-agent-growth.sh:1065` performs four download and four upload warmups, calling `close_idle_agent_transports` after every transfer. The actual admin handler (`proxy_qualification_test.go:568`) invokes `closeAll`; `closeEntriesWhere` and `retireLocked` remove the pool entries, rather than merely marking their sockets idle. Therefore a policy that prefers an eligible existing entry and otherwise uses ordinary round robin naturally falls back to round robin on every warmup. All four physical lanes still receive both directional warmups. The subsequent existing gate still requires four grown, quiescent receiving pools at both endpoints before retained/steady measurement.

The qualification route key excludes request method/path, so the download and upload requests can share the same eligible route transport after warming. No startup request count, synthetic demand, warmup-stage check, policy toggle, extra traffic or threshold exception is needed. This relies on ordinary pool retirement semantics that also apply to real administrative invalidation. It does not claim all four pools would naturally grow during a purely sequential production workload; unused lanes remain available for concurrency.

The original 96-row qualification validator has no postwarmup round-robin key pattern. Its exact schedule, both-direction pool growth, payload/event conservation, lifecycle, limits and each paired aggregate/legacy ratio of at least 0.90 remain required. The candidate server's generic public-route policy must apply equivalently in aggregate and legacy cohorts; aggregate-only improvement against an unaffected legacy selector is not the full production comparison.

By contrast, the later 48-row controller-policy validator explicitly requires sequences 9–12 to visit four distinct fresh keys, then sequences 13–16 to revisit the corresponding keys. Locality deliberately changes this behavior. Reusing that validator and ignoring its first-visit failures would be invalid. Any future diagnostic would require a separately reviewed routing-policy estimand and truthful lane/origin outcomes, while preserving the original qualification and historical verdicts. Do not manufacture four initial retained visits before enabling affinity.

## Smallest safe architecture to design

Use a public-route RoundTripper that chooses a pooled entry and increments its in-flight ownership atomically at **RoundTrip invocation**, under the pool mutex. Do not reserve at transport construction: the caller might never invoke RoundTrip. Do not select an apparently idle entry and increment later: concurrent callers could all select it before any count changes. Release ownership through the existing response-body EOF/Close/error lifecycle; do not hold a mutex during HTTP I/O or serialize requests behind the preferred lane.

Check at most four exact full-key candidates, excluding only the lane from the matching template. Preserve kind, agent ID, target/environment ID, origin/management URL, TLS verification and certificate identity, and response timeout. Require the same live agent generation, allowed live public lane, non-retired transport, zero current request ownership and advisory reusable state. Use deterministic preference based on an honestly named completion hint if recency is needed; current `lastUsed` cannot be described as completion. Ordinary retirement, expired or stale idle hints, errors and disconnected generations must fall back safely. Snapshot/revalidate lane ownership without introducing pool/tunnel lock inversion. All-busy and no-eligible-entry cases retain round-robin selection and existing admission queues, one-shot fallback, pooled lifetime accounting and draining behavior. Preserve nil-pool behavior with ordinary direct round-robin transport construction. Never wait for the preferred entry to become free.

Entry `inFlight` is not physical-lane occupancy: other route entries, one-shot requests and draining retired transports may still use that lane. Do not claim least-loaded-lane scheduling or guaranteed whole-lane idleness from this field. The narrow preference concerns this reusable route entry only. A reusable factory wrapper must not retain a single claimed entry across multiple RoundTrip calls; every invocation needs its own exactly-once body ownership.

Four lanes and their sessions remain provisioned. This is sequential locality, not a one-lane topology or concurrency cap. Busy/slow response bodies must cause concurrent work to spread through the existing fallback; a trickling attacker cannot gain new capacity, cross a route/TLS/security key or force trusted control onto a public lane. Preserve global/session/stream memory ceilings, allocation accounting, bounded pool entries, cancellation, peer-FIN lease ownership, control admission, route invalidation and agent-generation retirement. Avoid changing environment/control transports in the first source design unless an actual call-site dependency requires it.

The source review should be accompanied by focused authored tests for exact-key isolation, simultaneous selection, body lifetime and cancellation, retirement/generation races, all-busy fallback, four-lane concurrent use and unchanged warmup retirement behavior. Include stale idle hints and origin expiration; a hint is not an assertion that net/http must reuse a socket. These tests are a prerequisite for a later execution grant, not permission to run them now.

## Distinct cold-pool hypothesis

The closed early-share candidate had four initial-pool no-ops per role because a quarter of its 2 MiB initial pool is only 512 KiB. Its later successful 4 MiB grants therefore did not test whether an earlier reservation-backed cold-session increase prevents initial TCP threshold formation. That is a different, potentially riskier hypothesis; it must not be presented as retuning the failed no-reservation helper.

Existing `AggregateReceiveWindowGrowth.Grow` reserves memory before session-pool, allocation and byte publication, retains successful reservations across ambiguous errors, and releases only after session shutdown. A cold-pool intervention would consume additional global budget earlier and retain that cost for the entire session. Demand consumption alone does not make arbitrary public trickle/stall patterns harmless; four lanes, both endpoint directions and many sessions can amplify retained memory and admission denial. Denial must preserve usable existing credit, and partial publication must retain its leases. Existing caps are necessary but do not establish acceptable fairness or latency under earlier occupancy.

The sibling's retained same-owner extraction provides a useful disconfirmation: lane 1 (`:39288`) and lane 3 (`:39268`) still had effectively infinite ssthresh at approximately 1.846 and 1.839 seconds into their initial download warmups. The next same-wrapper growth call proves that the earlier successful 512 KiB-to-1 MiB grant had completed by 0.727 and 0.808 seconds, requiring at least a 4 MiB pool. Growth timestamps denote call start, so they do not independently establish completion at the larger later target sizes. Their first selected sequence-9 samples later show finite ssthresh 236 and 234. Thus the claim that the finite threshold was created while these owners were still constrained to their initial 2 MiB pools is contradicted. Broader historical influence of an early ramp remains possible, but it does not identify the threshold creator or justify a new reservation-backed intervention. There is no saved warmup-end TCP_INFO boundary; the transition could occur later in warmup, upload or intervening idle. Root accepts closing this cold-pool hypothesis as a next candidate. No new trace expansion, trigger/cap tuning or WAN run follows. For a future implementation, use the actual current production source and ordinary binaries, with new exact source/build receipts. The accepted diagnostic overlay is a mechanism reference, not a candidate base that should silently carry the closed early-share helper forward. Preserve the original 96-row workload and acceptance semantics while binding its new binaries honestly; never relabel historical provenance. The source-feasibility decision identifies no inherent conflict with that qualification, but does not predict it will pass.
